Perspectives in P5.js

What is it, how it works and which results is possible to get.

Here is our assignment focused on the topic of perspectives. Although I do not remember many details due to health complications I was facing at the time, I understood that our task was to "draw" entirely through JavaScript.
For this assignment, no laptops or devices were required. All we needed was a piece of paper and a pen or pencil. Within a specific time limit, we put all our focus into drafting our instructions. Once the time was up, we exchanged our algorithms to test each other’s work and share our unique approaches.

However, I feel it is important to mention that my initial expectations for this discipline were somewhat different. I anticipated that a first-semester introduction would cover core website design practices, such as UI/UX fundamentals and structural exercises, before launching into independent projects. When you lack prior coding experience, it can feel highly frustrating to be expected to create something complex and high-level in an unfamiliar territory, especially under tight deadlines and the pressure to make it fit for university evaluation.

If I could travel back in time to the beginning of the semester, I would suggest starting with a solid foundation in HTML and CSS first. Learning to build a basic webpage, then gradually making the visuals and user interfaces more complex, allows you to naturally see how your creative ideas translate into functional code. Only later, once everyone has mastered these solid basics, would we transition into JavaScript, generative art, and final projects.

Nevertheless, despite these initial hurdles, I managed to produce several examples of what can be achieved within the p5.js ecosystem for the Perspectives assignment.
