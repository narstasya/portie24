Physical Computing | Blinkenlichten Headphones

What is it, how it works and which results is possible to get.

We also had a course in Physical Computing. Initially, it sounded incredibly scary and challenging to me, and honestly, that is exactly how it turned out in the end, haha! We were given an Arduino board and a specific task: to write code that would run on the hardware, and to personally solder every single wire. While the conceptual idea was fascinating, linking physical components to active coding proved to be much harder than expected. 

I worked in a pair with a classmate, and we split the responsibilities to make the workflow more efficient. I took charge of the soldering and the programming, while my partner focused on the visuals — designing the physical appearance of the headphones and arranging the final look of the scene.

Our concept was to create a pair of headphones equipped with two blinking LED lights that would automatically flash whenever the sensor detected a loud ambient sound outside. Later on, everyone in our group presented their final functional installations.
